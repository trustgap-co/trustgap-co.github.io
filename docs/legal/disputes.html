1. Purpose

This Policy explains how TrustGap handles cancellations, delivery problems, item complaints, returns, refunds, and payment disputes. It applies together with the deal- specific terms, the seller’s lawful return policy, the payment provider’s rules, applicable courier terms, and non-excludable consumer rights.

TrustGap is not the seller of the item unless the deal says so. The seller remains responsible for the item and for legal remedies relating to the sale. TrustGap provides the transaction process and dispute channel described here.

2. Before payment

Review the item description, condition, price, fees, delivery information, seller details, inspection window, return conditions, and dispute rules before paying. If a material term is unclear, ask the seller or do not pay until it is clarified in the deal record.

3. Buyer cancellation before dispatch

A buyer may request cancellation before dispatch by using [CANCELLATION METHOD].

Whether the payment is refundable, and whether a disclosed reasonable charge applies, depends on the deal terms and applicable law. A seller must not use a hidden or undisclosed term to remove a consumer right.

Once dispatch has occurred, the buyer should follow the return or refusal process rather than attempting to cancel through an unrelated channel.

4. Problems covered by the dispute process

A buyer may open a dispute for a material problem such as:

  • non-delivery;
  • wrong item, quantity, size, or model;
  • material difference from the agreed description or condition;
  • counterfeit or suspected counterfeit item;
  • significant damage caused before or during delivery;
  • missing material component or accessory;
  • item that is materially defective or unsafe; or
  • another problem identified in the deal-specific rules.

A dispute is not a guarantee of a refund. TrustGap will assess the available evidence and the applicable terms and law.

5. Problems usually not covered

Subject to applicable consumer rights, disputes may not be accepted for:

  • a change of mind after a lawful sale where no cancellation or return right applies;
  • normal wear, minor cosmetic differences, or an expressly disclosed defect;
  • damage caused after handover by the buyer;
  • use, alteration, installation, or repair that caused the problem;
  • failure to follow reasonable care, inspection, or reporting instructions;
  • an issue raised after the stated deadline without a valid reason; or
  • a dispute based on false, manipulated, or incomplete evidence.

This section does not remove rights that cannot lawfully be excluded.

6. How to open a dispute

Open a dispute through the Report a problem button on your tracking page or contact within after the delivery event shown on the deal. Include:

  • deal ID and contact details;
  • a clear description of the problem;
  • photographs or video showing the item, packaging, label, serial number, and damage where relevant;
  • delivery and handover information;
  • messages or documents relevant to the agreed description; and
  • the remedy requested.

Do not send passwords, payment PINs, OTPs, or unnecessary identity documents.

7. Review process

TrustGap may acknowledge the dispute within . We may ask the buyer, seller, courier, or payment provider for information. The seller should respond within . If the seller does not respond, TrustGap may decide using the available evidence, subject to applicable law.

Possible outcomes include:

  • release of payment to the seller;
  • a partial refund or agreed adjustment;
  • a full refund;
  • return and refund;
  • redelivery or replacement;
  • denial of the dispute with reasons; or
  • referral to the seller, courier, payment provider, regulator, or court.

TrustGap is not required to decide in favour of the buyer or seller merely because one party is more vocal. We will apply the deal terms, evidence, policy, and law.

8. Returns

Do not return an item unless TrustGap or the seller provides return instructions. The instructions should state the return address, courier, time limit, packaging requirements, tracking requirement, and who pays the return charge.

The buyer must keep the item in the condition reasonably required by applicable law and the deal terms. A seller must not reject a lawful return through an undisclosed or unfair condition.

9. Refunds

Where a refund is approved, TrustGap will initiate or instruct the refund using the payment method or process permitted by the payment provider and applicable law. The time for the funds to appear may depend on the payment provider, bank, card scheme, or other financial institution.

Delivery fees, return fees, payment-processing fees, and TrustGap fees will be treated as shown in the deal terms and applicable law. Any non-refundable charge must be disclosed before payment and must not unlawfully remove a consumer right.

TrustGap will not promise an “instant refund” unless it controls the entire process and can reliably meet that promise.

10. Chargebacks and duplicate claims

A buyer should contact TrustGap and the payment provider promptly if a transaction is unauthorised or fraudulent. A chargeback through a card issuer or payment provider may operate separately from the TrustGap dispute process. The buyer must not seek double recovery for the same loss.

TrustGap and the seller may need to provide transaction, delivery, communication, and dispute evidence to the payment provider or card scheme. Payment-provider terms and deadlines may apply.

11. Fraudulent or abusive claims

TrustGap may suspend a user, withhold a payout, reject a claim, or refer conduct to a competent authority where it reasonably suspects false evidence, repeated abusive claims, collusion, refund abuse, chargeback abuse, account takeover, or other fraud. This does not prevent a user from exercising a lawful right.
