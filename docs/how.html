How it works

Agree. Secure. Deliver. Inspect. Release.

Every TrustGap deal follows the same published steps, so the buyer and the seller always know what happens next, and what happens if something goes wrong.

The five steps

From “we have a deal” to “everyone is satisfied”.

  1. 1. Create one clear deal link

    The seller turns the chat into a link: item, condition, quantity, price, pickup address, the buyer’s delivery address, who pays delivery and who pays the fees. The seller sees the courier price and their exact payout before sending it. A link can’t be edited: a change makes a new link and retires the old one.

  2. 2. The buyer reviews and pays

    The buyer sees the exact deal, the delivery address, every fee and the rules, then pays on the payment provider’s secure page. TrustGap saves a fingerprint of exactly what the buyer agreed to.

  3. 3. Payment secured, the seller dispatches

    When the payment is confirmed, the seller’s dashboard shows a clear go-ahead to book pickup, not a screenshot or a promise. The seller adds a packing note as evidence.

  4. 4. Tracked delivery and a private PIN

    The buyer receives a 4-digit delivery PIN by SMS and on their tracking page. The seller never sees it. The rider can only mark the parcel delivered with that PIN, which the buyer gives after checking the item.

  5. 5. Inspect, then release

    The inspection window starts at handover. If the buyer confirms or raises nothing, the seller’s payout is released. If the buyer reports a problem, the dispute process applies and the money stays held until it is decided.

Plain language

What “payment secured” means, and what it doesn’t.

It means

  • The payment provider has confirmed the buyer’s payment and TrustGap has checked the amount.
  • The money is recorded against this one deal and is not paid to the seller yet.
  • The seller may now dispatch, within the ship-by time on the deal.
  • The money is released or refunded only by the rules shown on the deal.

It does not mean

  • That the seller has been paid.
  • That the item is authentic or exactly as described. That is what the inspection window is for.
  • That delivery is guaranteed on a particular day.
  • That TrustGap is a bank. TrustGap coordinates the deal; a licensed payment provider processes the money.

Every status, explained

Both sides see the same status at the same time.

The buyer’s tracking page and the seller’s dashboard show these statuses with times, so nobody has to ask “has the money come?” or “where is my parcel?”.

  • Link createdThe seller has created the deal. Nothing has been paid. The link expires at the time shown on it.
  • Payment securedThe buyer has paid. The money is held under the deal rules. The seller is told to dispatch.
  • Pickup bookedThe seller has booked the courier and added a packing note. This is the dispatch go-ahead in action.
  • Picked upThe courier has the parcel.
  • Out for deliveryThe rider is on the way to the buyer.
  • DeliveredThe buyer checked the item and gave the rider their PIN. The inspection window starts now.
  • Inspection openThe buyer can confirm the item or report a hidden problem until the window closes.
  • ReleasedNo problem reported (or the buyer confirmed). The seller’s payout is sent to their verified account.
  • Attempt failedThe rider couldn’t complete delivery (buyer unreachable, wrong address). The courier tries again.
  • RefusedThe buyer refused the parcel at the door. A dispute opens automatically.
  • DisputedA problem was reported. The money stays held while TrustGap reviews the evidence.
  • ReturnedThe parcel went back to the seller.
  • RefundedMoney sent back to the buyer’s own bank account, minus only deductions shown in writing.

The inspection window

Check at the door. Then a little more time for hidden problems.

The window starts the moment the buyer gives the rider their PIN. Its length is shown on the deal before payment. It is currently .

  • Check what you can at the door: it’s the right item, model, size and colour, and it isn’t visibly damaged.
  • Only then give the rider your PIN.
  • Found a hidden problem afterwards? Use Report a problem on your tracking page before the window closes, with photos or video.

What counts as a valid problem

Wrong item, quantity, size or model · materially different from the description or condition · counterfeit · significant damage · missing parts · defective or unsafe.

Evidence that helps

Clear photos or a short video of the item, packaging and label; serial numbers where relevant; and the chat where the item was described. The seller’s packing note and the courier’s records are reviewed too.

Changing your mind about a correct item is not covered. See Returns, refunds & disputes.

When things go wrong

What happens if…

The seller doesn’t dispatch

Each deal says how many days the seller has to hand the item to the courier.

→ If the time passes without pickup, the buyer is refunded in full, automatically.

The buyer isn’t available

The rider records a failed attempt and the courier contacts the buyer to try again.

→ The money stays held. Repeated failures are handled under the Delivery Policy.

The address is wrong

The buyer confirms the delivery address the seller entered before paying, and is told not to pay if it’s wrong.

→ If the courier still can’t deliver, TrustGap contacts both sides to arrange a corrected address or a return.

The item is refused at the door

A dispute opens automatically and the parcel goes back to the seller.

→ Wrong item: full refund. Correct item refused: delivery and return costs may be deducted, shown in writing.

The item is wrong, fake or damaged

The buyer reports it within the inspection window with evidence.

→ If upheld, the item goes back to the seller and the buyer gets a full refund to their own account.

The buyer goes quiet

The buyer doesn’t confirm and doesn’t report a problem.

→ When the inspection window ends, the seller is paid automatically. No chasing.

How a return works

Don’t send anything back on your own. When a return is approved, TrustGap books it with the courier and tells both sides who pays. If the seller sent the wrong or faulty item, the seller pays. If the buyer refused a correct item, the return cost may come out of the refund. The seller confirms the item came back.

When the seller is paid

Once the release condition is met, the payout goes to the seller’s verified account in their own name. Timing: . Weekends, public holidays and bank checks can add time.
